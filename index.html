CASE FILE #001 🧠

Circuits, connectomics & Alzheimer's

What if Alzheimer's and psychiatric disorders are the same crime, in different chapters?

Psychiatry lives in symptom space. Alzheimer's research lives in late-stage markers — plaques, tangles — reliable for diagnosis, useless for origins.

My bet: early AD is a failure in firing homeostasis within cortico-hippocampal circuits — a network regulation problem wearing a molecular-pathology costume.

Depression, anxiety, chronic stress — maybe early, reversible versions of the same circuit failure that goes irreversible in AD.

Open question: do therapy, sleep, and drugs stabilize vulnerable circuits, or quietly make them more fragile? Still trying to answer that without getting laughed out of a conference.

CASE FILE #002 📐

What if a physicist looked at Alzheimer's?

Disease as a trajectory through state space. Maybe the complexity is a coordinate problem, not a biology problem.

Most models treat biology as static measurements. They miss how variables move together over time as one system.

Each person is a vector — genetics, biomarkers, imaging, behavior — evolving as a dynamical system. Nonlinear things often turn linear if you rotate the coordinates enough.

Yes, I did try to explain Alzheimer's with the same math a physicist uses for a pendulum. No, I'm not sorry. Steeper trajectories mean instability; flatter ones mean resilience.

⚡ CASE FILE #003

Can you harvest electricity from a brain?

The brain runs on real voltage. Can any of that go somewhere useful? A thought experiment that humbled me fast.

First instinct: brain as battery, neurons as wires, free energy for the taking.

Reality check: neural signals are traveling waves of local depolarization, not current in a wire. Ions move locally — nothing to tap in series.

Measuring information ≠ acquiring energy. EEG reads voltage at near-zero energy cost; actual harvesting yields femtowatts and probably breaks the neuron doing it.

Open question I still like: could engineered neurons export charge the way electrocytes do? Where's the line between "measuring" and "stealing"?
