Circuits, Connectomics & Alzheimer's

Psychiatric disorders and Alzheimer's may share an early substrate at the circuit level — long before symptoms appear.

Psychiatry has historically operated in symptom space. Neurodegeneration research defined Alzheimer's through late-stage markers — amyloid plaques, tau tangles — which are reliable for diagnosis but are endpoints, not origins.

My argument: early AD may reflect failures in firing homeostasis within cortico-hippocampal circuits. Disease reframed as a problem of network regulation rather than isolated molecular pathology.

Depression, anxiety, and chronic stress may represent early, reversible perturbations of systems that later undergo irreversible failure in AD. The circuit-centric view unifies psychiatry and neurodegeneration in a single mechanistic framework.

Central question: do psychiatric interventions — drugs, therapy, sleep, exercise — stabilize vulnerable circuits, or increase long-term fragility? Preventive logic: not just how circuits break, but how they're preserved across a lifespan.

📐

What if a physicist viewed AD?

Disease progression as a trajectory through high-dimensional biological state space. The apparent complexity may be a coordinate problem, not a biological one.

Most frameworks treat biological processes as static measurements or probabilistic associations. They miss how variables jointly evolve across time within structured biological systems.

Each individual is a vector of measurable variables — genetics, molecular biomarkers, circuit measures, structural imaging, behavioral states — evolving as a dynamical system.

From physics: nonlinear dynamics often become tractable through transformations where they appear linear. Amyloid accumulation, tau propagation, and synaptic dysfunction may harmonize across individuals in the right coordinate system.

Steeper trajectories signal accelerating circuit instability; flatter ones indicate resilience. AD as a long-term outcome of a system gradually approaching a regime of instability — shaped over decades.

Can you harvest electricity from the brain?

The brain generates real voltages. Can that energy go somewhere else? A thought experiment that sharpened my understanding of what neural electricity actually is.

Initial intuition: the brain as a weak battery, neurons as wires, signals harvestable by another system.

Refined: neural electricity isn't current in a wire. What propagates along an axon is a traveling wave of local membrane depolarization. Ions cross the membrane locally — they don't flow longitudinally as transferable current.

Key distinction: measuring information vs. acquiring energy. EEG and patch-clamp resolve voltage with near-zero energy extracted. Harvesting actually yields femtowatts at best, and disrupts neural function.

Remaining open questions: could synthetic biology redesign neurons to export charge like electrocytes? Where exactly is the boundary between minimally invasive measurement and energetically disruptive coupling?
